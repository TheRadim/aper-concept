Engineers first.

APER is a small workshop in Patras, Greece, where two mechanical engineers and their team design, program, machine and assemble bicycles.

It started with a thesis.

Nikos wrote his thesis at the Mechanical and Aeronautics Engineering Department of the University of Patras on a rear suspension for downhill bikes. He kept working on it after graduating.

Nikos

Co-founder, mechanical engineer

Developed the suspension idea that became the patented Rising Pivot, the heart of the KOMPace.

Giorgos

Co-founder, mechanical engineer

Saw where the idea could go and pushed to turn it into a complete enduro bike. Leads the product side today.

The team

Christos, Giorgos P. and Kostas

Joined as the workshop grew, across machining, assembly and support.

Named for a boar.

Aper is Latin for wild boar. In Greek myth the Erymanthian Boar lived in the mountains above Patras, the same mountains where our bikes are ridden and tested.

From billet to bike.

Six steps, one building. Scroll to watch a DRYAD take shape.

  1. Design
  2. Program
  3. Machine
  4. One-piece fork
  5. Join
  6. Build

  1. Design

    Every member is modelled around how a loaded bike is used on rough ground: where the load enters, where it travels, where it leaves. Weight is a result, not the target.

  2. Program

    The engineers who draw a part also write its toolpaths. A change to a rib goes from the model to the machine in the same room, often on the same day.

  3. Machine

    Each tube starts as a closed 7075-T6 aluminium profile. The mill cuts integrated ribs, varies the wall thickness and undercuts deep pockets. We call it RibLogic.

  4. One-piece fork

    The fork is cut from a single billet. Steerer, crown and blades are one part, with no bonded crown and no weld.

  5. Join

    Members lock together through machined interfaces, then are structurally bonded and bolted. The seatstays mount on replaceable bushings for controlled comfort.

  6. Build

    Every bike is assembled and checked in Patras to the specification you chose, then ridden before it ships.

What machining is not for.

Good engineering includes saying what a method doesn't promise.

The lightest frame
The DRYAD puts structural integrity and durability ahead of the lowest weight. A carbon race frame will weigh less.
The cheapest method
Machining removes much of the profile it starts from. What it buys is control over where every wall and rib goes.
One frame per rider
Rib design shapes how a member flexes, but each size is designed and tested as a whole, not customised per rider.
