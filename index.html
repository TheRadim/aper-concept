How a frame is made

See the full process

  1. Profile

    Each member starts as a closed-section profile of 7075-T6 aluminium.

  2. Program

    Toolpaths are written in-house, by the engineers who designed the part.

  3. Machine

    Ribs, variable walls and undercuts are cut where the load travels. APER calls it RibLogic.

  4. Fork

    The fork is cut from one billet: steerer, crown and blades in a single part.

  5. Assemble

    Members interlock, then are bonded and bolted. Seatstays mount on replaceable bushings.

Two engineers, one workshop

APER began with Nikos's thesis at the University of Patras: a rear suspension for downhill bikes.

Giorgos saw where it could go. The idea became the patented Rising Pivot and APER's first bike, the KOMPace. Christos, Giorgos P. and Kostas have since joined the team.

Aper is Latin for wild boar, after the Erymanthian Boar of Greek myth. The Erymanthos mountains rise above Patras, and they are where the bikes are ridden and tested.
