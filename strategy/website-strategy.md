# Website strategy

## 1. Who buys a Dryad? [A — validate with Giorgos's order data]
- **The experienced gravel / bikepacking rider (30–55), Northern & Central Europe.** Owns 2–4 bikes, reads BIKE/TOUR/Radavist, has done multi-day trips, values durability and serviceability over grams. Price-sensitive enough to notice €1,245 for a machined frameset.
- **The engineer / maker-minded cyclist.** Attracted by the method itself. Will read every spec. Likely the earliest adopters.
- **KOMPace enduro rider** — separate audience, same trust questions. Kept in the IA, not the focus of this concept.

## 2. Why the Dryad is desirable
Unusual construction you can see; a one-piece machined fork; comfort via a serviceable bushing; mounts everywhere; built in-house; transparent, fair pricing.

## 3. Why choose it over a conventional bike
Not "better than carbon" — **different and accountable**: you know who made it, how, and how to service it. Avoid unsupported claims (stiffer, lighter, stronger).

## 4. Buyer concerns → where the site answers them
| Concern | Answer on the site |
|---|---|
| "Will this tiny company exist in 5 years?" | People with names, workshop, 5-yr warranty, crash replacement, spares shop |
| "Bonded aluminium — will it hold?" | Plain explanation of interlock + bond + bolt; testing info **[needs data from APER]** |
| "Which size? No dealer near me." | Size finder from published rider heights; contact the founders |
| "No returns?" | Stated clearly, with the reason (built to order) and the alternative (talk to us first) |
| "When do I get it?" | Deposit → confirm build → production → delivery from Dec 2026 |
| "Can I ride one?" | **[needs answer — test bikes / demo days]** |
| "Weight / clearance?" | Publish them **[needs confirmation]** |

## 5. First 10 seconds
*This is the DRYAD, a gravel and bikepacking bike machined from aluminium by APER in Patras, Greece. Frameset €1,245, complete from €2,520. Reserve with €400.*

## 6. Conversion actions (in priority)
1. **Reserve a DRYAD** (€400 deposit) — existing WooCommerce product.
2. **Ask the founders** (email/contact form) — the human fallback for every concern above.
3. **Find your size** — micro-conversion leading to 1 or 2.
4. **Get build updates** (newsletter) — for the not-yet-ready.

## 7. E-commerce recommendation
Keep WooCommerce for deposits and spares — it works. **Don't build a configurator-checkout.** Instead the build selector on the Dryad page hands its choice (size + build + price) to the reserve link / email. Spares stay a simple shop list.

## 8. What APER can realistically maintain
- 3 core pages + 2 bike pages + simple Journal (3–4 posts a year: launch, factory, a customer trip).
- Data (prices, specs, geometry) in one structured place.
- No blog cadence, no social embeds, no live chat.

- **B — WordPress theme by me:** custom block theme replacing Elementor on the marketing pages; WooCommerce untouched. Recommended.
